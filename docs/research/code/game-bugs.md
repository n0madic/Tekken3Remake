# Game bugs and quirks

This is a register of defects found in the original code (Japan Rev.1; USA identical unless noted), for a remake that offers a faithful mode and an optional "fixed" mode. Each entry gives:

- where the defect is;
- what the original does, which the ports in `tools/research` reproduce bit for bit;
- the visible effect;
- a suggested fix.

Entries marked **keep** change the feel of the game when fixed; they should stay on in the faithful mode and be considered carefully for the fixed mode.

Status: every entry is `confirmed` from code; entries with a port are also confirmed by the harness.

## Gameplay-visible

| # | Where | Original behaviour | Effect | Suggested fix |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `FUN_80063FE8` (camera look point, used by `CameraBlend` for the pairs 0+1, 0+2, 0+4) | The distance to target 1 uses target 0's `z` (`(T0.z − z)²` in both sums). | The look point of a blended camera is slightly off when the fighters are separated in `z` (side steps), so blends between the fight camera and throw or hit cameras drift a little. | Use `(T1.z − z)²` for the second distance. |
| 2 | `CameraFrameFighters` (`0x800650F8`) | The screen projections divide by the target depth without a check; a target exactly in the camera plane divides by zero, which the R3000 answers with −1 or +1. | For one frame the edge position of that fighter is ±1 pixel, so the framing may jump. | 3 | `FUN_800B4BE4` (Tekken Ball CPU, attack filter) | Player 1 runs the filter when the ball is more than 2,000 units ahead; player 2 when the ball is ahead but **closer** than 2,000 units. | The two CPU sides play Tekken Ball differently. | Mirror player 1's test for player 2 (ball more than 2,000 units ahead). |

| 6 | `CameraBlendPair` (`0x80064458`) | When the blended projection distance `H` is not positive, source 3's `H` is left as uninitialised stack. | Undefined field of view for one frame. It cannot happen with the shipped `H` values (all positive). | Keep the previous `H`. |

| 17 | `AiCollectCandidates` (`0x80056A4C`), marking pass | Besides candidates whose target move has state bit 23, it marks as unusable (`-1`) every candidate whose target equals the stack word `sp+0x1C`, which the routine never writes; it holds whatever an earlier call left there. | None in practice. | 18 | `AiCollectCandidates`, look-ahead into the default continuation | The branch condition of an ordinary row is evaluated on register `$s2`: the list start, or the row after the last common-row block, instead of the row itself (common rows use the right row). | Look-ahead candidates are kept or dropped by the wrong row's condition, so the CPU sometimes plans a follow-up that will not be open, or skips one that will. | Evaluate `BranchCondition` on the row being tested. |
| 19 | `AiExecuteCandidate` (`0x80058D6C`) | The routine draws `k` uniformly below the marked count and walks the candidate table. The direction alternates with bit 0 of record `+0x02` (a counter that advances whenever the fighter's pose frame changes). Backwards it stops when `k < 0` (uniform). Forwards it stops when `k ≤ 0`, so `k = 0` and `k = 1` both pick the first marked candidate. | On about half the frames, the first marked candidate is twice as likely and the last marked candidate can never be chosen (with two or more marked). This biases CPU move choice towards moves listed early in the branch list. | Stop on `k < 0` in both directions. |
| 20 | `FUN_80058ED0` (branch-to-slot test, used by King's hook and `AiUpdate`) | After walking the running move's branch list, the default continuation is read from the row after the last walked row. | 21 | `FUN_800582A0` (guard and evasion rows, reaction 2) | The routine marks guard rows in several passes, but when its last pass (crouch dashes when approached fast at close range) runs, it returns only that pass's count. | When the CPU is rushed down it may have marked guard rows yet see a count of 0; it then crouch-backs instead of executing them. | Return the sum of all passes. |
| 23 | `AiUpdate`, reaction 1 against a repeated move | The CPU answers a move that already hurt it by comparing the opponent's move with slot 0 of its 8-entry damage history (`+0x1DC`), not with every entry or the newest one. | The "learned" counter works only for the move stored in slot 0, which is replaced every eighth hit. | Compare with every history entry (or the last written one). |

| 58 | `HitVibration` (`0x80075CBC`) | The pad index for the KO flag (pattern `0x0B`) starts at −1 and is set only by a clean hit (the attacker, `+0x22`) or throw damage (the partner, `+0x21`) in the same frame. ## Arithmetic quirks

| # | Where | Original behaviour | Effect | Suggested fix |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | `LaunchTrajectory` (`0x8002F2C8`) **keep** | The flight time divides a possibly negative sum as an unsigned 32-bit number and continues with wrapped arithmetic. | High bounces (launchers from a low height) get their arc from the wrapped value; juggle timing depends on it. | Keep for authenticity. A clean version must reproduce the same arcs, or combos change. |
| 8 | `IkSolveLimb`, arm bend (see [animation.md](../formats/animation.md#pose-to-matrices)) | `inv · 0x9204` overflows a 32-bit int for target distances `L` = 72–74 (closer targets get no bend). | A visibly wrong elbow bend when an IK hand target lies at that distance. | Use a 64-bit product. |
| 9 | `SegmentHitsCylinder` (`0x800479A4`) **keep** | The perpendicular-foot products wrap to 32 bits for long segments far from the cylinder axis. | Rare false hits or misses of long swept segments. | Use 64-bit products; this can change hit results in rare cases. |
| 41 | Tekken Force enemy slots (`FUN_800B1778`) | The slot timers (`+0xC`, `+0x14`) are 16-bit (`lh`) copies of the level clock `0x800B70D4` (and of the text-frame count `0x800B6AE4`), compared with the 32-bit clocks. Both clocks restart with each level. | After 32,708 running frames in one level (about 9 minutes, possible by farming a spawn pattern, since each kill adds time), every timer reads as already expired: `+n SEC` no longer appears, defeated enemies skip their 60-frame wait and 40-frame blink, and new models load without the 2-frame wait. | Keep the timers as 32-bit values. |

## Faithful versus fixed mode

The ports in `tools/research` reproduce entries 1–25 and 52–58 (the fight harness `fight_harness.py` patches 1, 53, 54, 55, 58 and 59 in RAM, so its traces are those of the fixed code; with `gameplay_fixes` it also patches 7, 8 and 52 and replaces `SegmentHitsCylinder` with the exact version of 9 for the fixed trace set), the screen entries 26–32 (`screens_sim.py`), 35 (`menu_sim.py`), 36–37 (`theater_sim.py`), 39 (`command_list_sim.py`) and the arcade's 62 (`verify_arcade_sky.py`). Entry 38 is unread data, reproduced by not reading it. A fixed mode should switch the entries individually. Entries 7 and 9 alter hit and juggle behaviour and should stay faithful by default. Entries 1–6, 8, 16, 18 and 20 are safe to fix; entries 17, 19, 21 and 23 change CPU choices slightly and are safe as well. Enabling entry 10 changes the CPU's style substantially. Entries 26–28, 32, 35–37, 49–51, 62, 66, 67 and 68 only affect presentation; a remake can fix them freely (the remake keeps 67 as the arcade draws it and retouches 68). Entries 29–31 are unlock and opponent-choice rules; 29 sets the unlock pace and should stay faithful by default. Entries 53–56 only affect the camera and a glow, 58 a pad vibration, 59 a knocked-out CPU's input; 52 changes tap inputs and is gameplay. Entries 33, 34, 39, 40 and 41 are latent (41 needs a nine-minute Tekken Force level); 42, 43, 46, 47 and 48 have no known effect; 44 and 45 depend on register or stack contents (`ball_sim.py` takes the start-up `$s7` as a parameter). The Tekken Force port (`force_sim.py`) reproduces 41.

**Remake policy.** The remake plan classifies every entry ([remake-plan.md](../../remake-plan.md#original-bugs)). Entries without effect on balance or gameplay are not reproduced. Gameplay-affecting entries (3, 7, 8, 9, 18–21, 23, 24, 29–31, 41, 52, 60) stay by default and are fixed together by one "Gameplay fixes" option; the remake implements every one of them (`RuleSet`, M5). A new entry added here must also be classified there.
